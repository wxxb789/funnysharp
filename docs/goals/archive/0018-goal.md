/goal
Make asynchronous work a first-class, composable part of FunnySharp without enlarging C#'s unavoidable async coloring or introducing another execution runtime. Completion means synchronous functions, Task, ValueTask, IAsyncEnumerable<T>, CancellationToken, TimeProvider, and relevant BCL streaming or coordination primitives participate in one predictable conceptual grammar, while signatures continue to reveal when work is asynchronous. Common workflows can stream rather than materialize, propagate backpressure, apply bounded concurrency, choose ordered or completion-order results, race or select first success safely, and choose fail-fast or error-accumulating behavior without boolean-policy ambiguity. No operation may perform sync-over-async, implicit Task.Run, unbounded fan-out, repeated ValueTask consumption, orphaned work, swallowed exceptions, or conversion of cancellation into a domain failure. Losing work after a race, stopping after failure, draining already-started operations, disposing resources, and combining simultaneous failures must all have deliberate and tested contracts. I/O-oriented APIs must add measurable workflow value through reduced copying, buffering, allocation, coordination overhead, or improved bounded concurrency; FunnySharp must leave an already-clear and optimal BCL operation unwrapped rather than claim to make an individual system call intrinsically faster. Evidence includes deterministic xUnit v3 tests for scheduling-independent semantics, cancellation and cleanup, streaming and backpressure tests, and reproducible latency, throughput, allocation, and concurrency measurements against equivalent BCL implementations. A custom scheduler, fiber system, or large IO monad is excluded.
