/goal
Determine whether reusable tree traversal, rewriting or zipper-style editing gives FunnySharp consumers a meaningful improvement over direct recursion, iterative visitors and existing .NET tree APIs for a real configuration, syntax or expression-tree task. Completion is a maintainer-ready decision with an executable consumer and equivalent reference behavior, identifying the smallest useful capability rather than reproducing a recursion-scheme or optics hierarchy. Evidence must address deep structures without accidental stack exhaustion, traversal order, failure locations, short-circuiting, unchanged-node reuse and the costs of rebuilding changed paths. Explicitly distinguish trees, shared DAGs and cyclic graphs; identity, duplicate visits, cycles and caller-supplied equality must have a supported or rejected meaning. Reusing nodes must not imply deep immutability when their contents remain mutable, and any zipper must have clear navigation, edit and root-reconstruction semantics. Judge total consumer-code, maintainability, allocation and copying on the disclosed workload, including the adapter needed to describe the application's own node types. Keep ordinary C# models and BCL child collections rather than requiring a replacement tree universe, and reuse Result, Validation or Location where they improve errors. The maintainer can adopt a narrow operation, retain a documented application pattern, defer or reject the capability based on the evidence. This research is independent of the compiled-pipeline and incremental-computation proposals and does not promise a general-purpose AST optimizer or automatic persistence.
